# Final Response Appendix

## Source Notes For The Main Draft

### 1. Atheism, Naturalism, And Scientism

The main draft uses a careful distinction:

- atheism can mean lack of belief in gods
- naturalism is a broader philosophical position
- scientism is the stronger claim that science is the only or final route to truth

That distinction is supported by the philosophy notes and by the SEP entries on atheism and naturalism. It is important because it prevents the draft from treating every atheist as if they were automatically a metaphysical naturalist.

### 2. Science And Its Presuppositions

The draft says science depends on reason, logic, the uniformity of nature, and observation.

This is a philosophical point, not a scientific discovery. The point is not that science is broken. The point is that science does not generate its own starting assumptions.

### 3. Consciousness

The draft keeps this modest on purpose.

The scientifically safe claim is that consciousness is closely linked to the brain. The philosophical claim is that correlation does not fully explain subjective awareness, first-person perspective, or rational reflection.

That is why the draft says the issue remains philosophically live rather than saying consciousness has not been shown to arise from physical processes at all.

### 4. Morality

The draft does not claim that atheism cannot explain moral behavior. It says that if morality is reduced to preference, convenience, or survival strategy, then moral obligation becomes difficult to ground.

That is a moral realism argument, not a claim that every atheist denies morality.

### 5. Fine-Tuning

The draft deliberately uses modest language.

Fine-tuning is treated as an inference: if the universe is strikingly life-permitting, design is one possible explanation. That is a philosophical best-explanation argument, not a scientific proof.

### 6. Christian Posture

The biblical passages are used for posture and framing:

- `1 Peter 3:15-16` and `2 Timothy 2:24-26` for gentleness and patience
- `Acts 17:22-31` for public reasoning with a non-Christian audience
- `Romans 1:18-25` and `Psalm 19:1-4` for general revelation and creation witness
- `1 Corinthians 15:3-8` for the resurrection summary

These texts should not be used as proof-texts to short-circuit the argument.

### 7. Resurrection

The historical case should stay cumulative. A careful version does not say "history proves resurrection." It says the resurrection is the best explanation of a cluster of historical claims and early Christian testimony.

The source plan treats N. T. Wright, Habermas/Licona, and Licona as the main background references for that style of argument.

## What To Keep In The Main Draft

- the atheism/naturalism distinction
- the science/scientism distinction
- the consciousness question
- the moral realism question
- the fine-tuning inference
- the historical Jesus case as a cumulative argument
- the gentleness-and-patience posture

## What To Keep Out Of The Main Draft

- any claim that all atheists are naturalists
- any claim that science proves or disproves God
- any claim that consciousness is not physically correlated
- any claim that fine-tuning is decisive proof
- any claim that the resurrection is uncontested historical fact
