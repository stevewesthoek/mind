# Argument Structure: Assurance, Sovereignty, and the Role of Works

## Position 1 Core Argument: God's Sufficiency

### Premise Chain
1. **God is sovereign and all-knowing** (Romans 9:15-20, Ephesians 1:4-5)
2. **God foreordains all things, including salvation** (Ephesians 1:3-14)
3. **Justification is God's unilateral forensic act** (Romans 3:21-26, Romans 5:1)
4. **If salvation depends on my works, it's not God's work—it's mine** (Ephesians 2:8-10)
5. **If it's my work, I can lose it, and I can boast about it** (Romans 4:4-5)
6. **Therefore, eternal security must be God's guarantee, not my achievement**
7. **Conclusion: Believers cannot lose eternal salvation because God's power guarantees it**

### Supporting Evidence
- **John 5:24** — Eternal life is a present possession for believers ("has eternal life")
- **John 10:27-29** — "No one can snatch them out of my Father's hand"
- **Romans 8:29-30** — Unbroken chain: foreknown → predestined → called → justified → glorified
- **Philippians 1:6** — "He who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion"
- **1 Peter 1:3-5** — "Inheritance... kept in heaven for you... guarded by God's power"

### Logic
**Modus tollens on loss of salvation:**
- IF believers can lose salvation, THEN God's foreknowledge is contingent (uncertain)
- God's foreknowledge is not contingent (Scripture affirms it)
- THEREFORE believers cannot lose salvation

## Position 2 Core Argument: Warnings and Perseverance

### Premise Chain
1. **The NT contains serious warnings against apostasy** (Hebrews 3:12-19, 1 Corinthians 10:1-13)
2. **Warnings are meaningless if the warned outcome is impossible** (Logical principle)
3. **Therefore, apostasy must be possible, or warnings are vacuous**
4. **OT types provide typological instruction** (1 Corinthians 10:11)
5. **The OT shows believers losing covenant status through unfaithfulness**
6. **Therefore, the same pattern applies to NT believers under the new covenant**
7. **Conclusion: Believers must persevere in faith or risk apostasy**

### Supporting Evidence
- **Hebrews 3:12-14** — "See to it... that none of you falls away"
- **Hebrews 10:26-27** — "If we deliberately keep on sinning... only a fearful expectation of judgment"
- **1 Corinthians 10:1-13** — Israel's experience as warning: many fell in the wilderness
- **Jude 5** — "The Lord... destroyed those who did not believe"
- **Romans 11:20-22** — "Do not be arrogant, but tremble... God's kindness and severity"

### Logic
**Argument from rhetorical purpose:**
- Warnings have pragmatic force (they change behavior)
- Warnings are meaningless if outcome is impossible
- Therefore, outcomes must be possible
- Therefore, apostasy is possible
- Therefore, perseverance matters

## Critical Tension: How to Interpret Apostasy

### Position 1 Resolution: Apostasy Reveals Non-Election

**1 John 2:19:**
> "They went out from us, but they did not really belong to us. For if they had belonged to us, they would have remained with us."

**Implication:** Those who "fall away" were never truly elected/justified.

**Support:**
- Apostasy reveals hidden state; doesn't change actual state
- Matthews 7:21-23 — "I never knew you" (they were never truly his)
- 2 Timothy 2:18-19 — "The Lord knows those who are his"

**Strength:** This makes warnings consistent with sovereignty (warnings screen/reveal the real from the false).

**Weakness:** Requires denying that any truly regenerate believer can apostasize.

---

### Position 2 Resolution: Apostasy Reveals Lost Covenant Status

**Hebrews 10:26-27 (as read by Position 2):**
> "If we deliberately keep on sinning... only a fearful expectation of judgment remains"

**Implication:** True believers can reject faith and lose standing.

**Support:**
- Hebrews 5:11-6:6 — "Falling away" is presented as real possibility
- Ephesians 5:5-6 — No inheritance for those in certain behaviors
- Romans 11:20-22 — Gentiles could be "cut off" from the covenant

**Strength:** Takes warnings at face value (they describe real possibilities).

**Weakness:** Conflicts with Romans 8:29-30 (unbroken chain implies unbroken security).

## The Baptism Subclaim

### Position 1: Baptism as Sign

- Baptism is an **ordinance** (command to obey)
- Baptism is a **sign** (testifies to faith already present)
- Baptism is an **act of obedience** (not a means of grace)
- **Evidence:** Acts 10:44-48 (Spirit before baptism); 1 Corinthians 1:17 (Paul not sent to baptize but to preach); Colossians 2:11-12 (real circumcision is Christ's, baptism depicts it)

### Position 2: Baptism as Sacrament

- Baptism is a **sacrament** (means of grace)
- Baptism is an **efficacious act** (produces grace, not just signifies it)
- Baptism is **necessary for salvation** (or at least for full remission)
- **Evidence:** Acts 2:38 ("for the remission of sins"); Romans 6:3-11 (baptism into Christ's death); Acts 22:16 (Ananias: "wash away your sins")

### Critical Passage: Acts 10:44-48

**What it says:** The Holy Spirit falls on Cornelius and family *before* Peter commands baptism.

**Position 1 reading:** Separates Spirit-reception from baptism; Spirit is the reality, baptism is the sign that follows.

**Position 2 reading:** Exception that proves the rule; doesn't overturn Acts 2:38's general command.

**Neutral observation:** The passage shows the Spirit *can* come before baptism, but doesn't prove baptism is always a sign-only.

## The Sovereignty Question (Most Fundamental)

**What both sides must answer:**

**If God foreknows all things:**
- Does He foreknow who will apostasize?
- If yes, did He foreknow they would apostasize while foreordaining their salvation?
- Can foreknown apostasy be "real" if it's foreknown?

**Position 1's answer:**
- God foreknows who will truly be saved and who will falsely profess
- True apostasy (actual loss) is foreknown to be *impossible*
- False profession (apostasy that reveals non-election) is foreknown to occur
- God's foreknowledge includes both the means and the ends

**Position 2's answer:**
- God foreknows but does not foreforce outcomes
- Believers are free to apostasize; God knows if they will but doesn't make them will it
- Compatibilism: foreknowledge and human freedom coexist

**Philosophical tension:** Compatibilism (freedom + foreknowledge) vs. theological tension (if foreknown, how truly free?).

---

## Common Ground

Both positions affirm:
1. **Christ's blood is the sufficient payment** for all sins (Hebrews 9-10)
2. **Faith in Christ is essential** to salvation (Ephesians 1:13, Romans 3:22)
3. **God is completely sovereign** (Psalms 115:3, Daniel 4:35)
4. **Obedience matters** in the Christian life (1 Peter 1:14-16, Hebrews 12:14)
5. **Judgment and reward are real** (2 Corinthians 5:10, Revelation 20:12-13)
6. **Wilson is saved** (both agree this is the practical conclusion)

## Where the Positions Actually Differ

| Point | Position 1 | Position 2 |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| **Can a truly regenerate believer lose salvation?** | No | Yes (or at least, they're unsure) |
| **What do apostasy cases teach?** | Non-election revealed | Salvation truly lost |
| **What is the force of NT warnings?** | Sanctification, self-examination | Real threat of loss |
| **Is baptism necessary for salvation?** | No (necessary to obey, not to save) | Yes (or at minimum, very important) |
| **Do OT types bind us?** | Typologically (Christologically fulfilled) | Typologically + prescriptively |
| **What does "sealed" mean in Ephesians 1:13?** | Permanent guarantee | Provisional covering |
| **Can God's foreknowledge change?** | No, it's immutable | No, but allows real human choice |

---

## Rhetorical Observations

1. **Position 1 is more internally logically consistent** but requires affirming that all apostasy cases were never truly saved.

2. **Position 2 respects the force of biblical warnings** but struggles to reconcile them with sovereignty doctrines.

3. **Both sides use 1 John 2:19 but read it differently:**
   - Position 1: "They apostasize because they were never truly his"
   - Position 2: "They could have been his but left; their departure proves it"

4. **The debate is ultimately about the nature of foreknowledge and freedom**, not primarily about baptism.

5. **Both sides agree that Wilson is safe**, which suggests the practical import of the debate is limited for believers (but not for doctrinal clarity).
