# Legal Gospel Framework Expanded From Source

## Core thesis

Source idea: God made the world perfect, Adam sinned, the world fell, and we are born into sin through Adam. The gospel is God’s legal answer to human guilt.

Clarity expansion: The problem is not just broken behavior. The problem is inherited sin, real guilt, and judgment before God.

**Scripture support**

- Genesis 1:31
- Genesis 3:1-19
- Romans 5:12
- Romans 6:23

**Source needs**

- None for the basic framework.
- If this is turned into an outreach letter, add explicit verses for creation, fall, and inherited sin.

## Morality and judgment

Source idea: When you make a judgment you make yourself a judge. If you judge, the question is whose law and by which authority.

Clarity expansion: Moral language already assumes law. Law assumes authority. Authority assumes accountability.

**Scripture support**

- Romans 2:14-16
- Genesis 18:25
- Psalm 24:1
- Romans 3:19-20

**Source needs**

- Philosophical support for objective moral realism if this is addressed to an atheist audience.

## Law and authority

Source idea: God is called Judge because He is the Creator of the law.

Clarity expansion: God is not subject to a higher moral court. His righteousness is the source of the standard.

**Scripture support**

- Isaiah 33:22
- Deuteronomy 32:4
- Psalm 9:7-8
- James 4:12

**Source needs**

- A clearer distinction between God as lawgiver and human legal authorities.

## Courtroom structure

Source idea: Legal terminology helps explain the gospel.

Clarity expansion:

- Judge: God
- Law: God’s holy standard
- Accuser: Satan
- Defendant: sinful humanity
- Witness: conscience
- Advocate: Jesus Christ

**Scripture support**

- Job 1:6-12
- Zechariah 3:1-5
- Revelation 12:10
- Romans 2:15
- 1 John 2:1

**Source needs**

- None.

## Sin and guilt

Source idea: Breaking God’s law brings guilt and death.

Clarity expansion: Sin is not merely imperfection. It is transgression, rebellion, and culpable guilt.

**Scripture support**

- 1 John 3:4
- Romans 3:23
- Isaiah 59:2
- James 4:17

**Source needs**

- If the audience is skeptical, define guilt in legal rather than emotional terms.

## Blood, life, and payment

Source idea: The wages of sin is death. Breaking God’s law costs life. Blood stands for life given.

Clarity expansion: The Bible treats blood as a life-token in sacrificial language. The death penalty underlines the seriousness of sin.

**Scripture support**

- Genesis 2:17
- Leviticus 17:11
- Romans 6:23
- Hebrews 9:22

**Source needs**

- Avoid speculative statements about blood beyond the text.

## Love as sacrifice

Source idea: Love is sacrificial. It gives itself for another.

Clarity expansion: Love is not mainly a mood. It is a willing self-gift ordered toward another’s good.

**Scripture support**

- John 15:13
- Romans 5:8
- Ephesians 5:2
- 1 John 4:9-10

**Source needs**

- None.

## Jesus as substitute

Source idea: God gave His only begotten Son to die in our place so sinners could be saved.

Clarity expansion: Jesus is sinless, so He can stand for sinners. His death is substitutionary, not accidental.

**Scripture support**

- Isaiah 53:4-6
- Hebrews 4:15
- 2 Corinthians 5:21
- Galatians 3:13
- 1 Peter 2:24

**Source needs**

- If used in dialogue, explain “substitute” in plain language immediately.

## Resurrection as legal vindication

Source idea: Because Jesus is sinless, death had no hold on Him. God raised Him without breaking His own law.

Clarity expansion: The resurrection is not a separate topic. It is the public verdict that Jesus’ death was accepted and that He is righteous.

**Scripture support**

- Acts 2:24
- Romans 4:25
- 1 Corinthians 15:3-8

**Source needs**

- Historical case support if the reader rejects resurrection on naturalistic assumptions.

## Why other religions fail

Source idea: Other religions make people essentially good and tell them to do works to find rest or peace.

Clarity expansion: Any system that starts with human goodness and human merit misses the scale of guilt before a holy Judge.

**Scripture support**

- Romans 3:10-12
- Romans 3:19-20
- Ephesians 2:8-9
- Titus 3:5

**Source needs**

- If this is used publicly, avoid caricaturing specific religions. State the principle, then name the contrast.

## Why atheism cannot ground morality

Source idea: Moral judgment assumes a law, and law implies authority.

Clarity expansion: Naturalism can describe behavior, preference, and survival. It cannot easily supply binding moral obligation.

**Scripture support**

- Romans 2:14-16
- Genesis 18:25
- Romans 3:19-20

**Source needs**

- Philosophical backing if this becomes a focused atheist-facing section.

## Questions still to answer

Source idea: Why did God allow the serpent? What about evil? What about other gods? What about the Bible’s unity? What about translation confidence?

Clarity expansion: These are not distractions. They are follow-up questions that sit behind the legal-gospel framework.

**Scripture support**

- Genesis 3:1-24
- Job 1:6-12
- Romans 5:12-21
- 2 Timothy 3:16-17

**Source needs**

- Separate notes are needed for evil, divine permission, textual reliability, and spiritual warfare.
