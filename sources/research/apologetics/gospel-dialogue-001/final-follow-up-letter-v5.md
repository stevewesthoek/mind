# The Gospel in Plain Language

---

## Start Here: You Already Believe in a Law

Before we talk about God, Jesus, or the cross — let's talk about you.

When you see a child being hurt, something inside you says: *that is wrong.*

Not just "I don't like that." Wrong. As in: it should not happen. It is a violation of something real.

That reaction is important. The moment you say something is *truly wrong* — not just personally annoying, but actually wrong — you are appealing to a law above you. A law you didn't make. A law that applies to everyone.

Here is the question: where does that law come from?

If you make up your own moral rules, then your rules are just your opinion. And someone else's rules are just their opinion. And nobody is actually wrong. But you don't really believe that. You believe some things are *actually* wrong.

Every human being does.

This is not a small thing. It is the key to understanding the gospel.

Every religion on earth is ultimately about morality. Every religion asks: *how do I become good enough?* Islam asks you to submit and obey. Buddhism asks you to discipline your desires. Hinduism tracks your actions across lifetimes. Every system puts the burden back on you.

Christianity is different. Christianity says: the debt is already there, and you cannot pay it. But someone already paid it for you.

That is what this letter is about.

---

## Where Morality Comes From

God is the Creator of the universe. He made everything that exists — including you.

The Bible says:

> *"In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth."* (Genesis 1:1)

Plain English: Everything that exists came from God.

Because God made you, He has the right to set the standard for how you should live. This is not random. You wouldn't tell the person who built a house that they have no right to set rules for how it's used. God made you. He knows how you were designed to live.

The moral law you feel inside — the one that says *this is wrong, that is right* — is God's law, built into every human being. You were made in His image. That is why every person in every culture throughout history has had a sense of right and wrong. It did not come from evolution or society. It came from the One who made you.

---

## What Went Wrong

God made the world perfect. No death. No suffering. No shame. Human beings had a direct relationship with God — open, free, close. No barrier between them.

Then God gave one command. He said:

> *"Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die."* (Genesis 2:16-17)

Plain English: You can have everything — just don't take this one thing. If you do, you will die.

The serpent — identified in Scripture as Satan — came to Eve and said: *you won't die. If you eat it, you'll become like God, knowing good and evil for yourself.*

That was the offer: stop receiving your moral standard from God. Become your own standard.

They ate.

That is the fall. And it explains everything you see wrong in the world today — disease, injustice, cruelty, death, decay. This is not what God designed. This is the result of humanity taking moral authority into its own hands.

Look around. We are doing a terrible job running our own lives and our own world. That is the fall in action.

---

## The Legal Problem

Here is the situation clearly.

God is holy. That means He is completely separate from everything impure or evil. He cannot simply look away when wrong is done. A judge who ignores crimes is not merciful — he is corrupt. God is not corrupt.

The Bible says:

> *"The wages of sin is death."* (Romans 6:23)

Plain English: Sin earns a payment. That payment is death.

Wages are what you earn. Sin earns death. This is not God being cruel. Cutting yourself off from the source of all life produces death — just like cutting off oxygen produces suffocation. The result follows from the act.

So here is the problem. Every person has sinned. Not just big obvious things — the fall means that all of us have lived as our own moral authority, deciding for ourselves what is right and wrong, answering to no one above us. That is the core of it.

The debt is real. It is a life debt. And you cannot pay it with money, good behavior, religion, or effort. The debt is your life.

---

## Why Blood? Why Is That the Price?

This is the part most people miss.

Life is in the blood. Physically and spiritually, blood equals life. The Bible states this directly:

> *"For the life of the flesh is in the blood."* (Leviticus 17:11)

Plain English: Blood is where life is. They are the same thing.

Because sin earns death — the loss of life — the only payment that can cancel the debt is life. Blood.

Here is how you know this is a real principle and not just a religious idea: every dark religion throughout history has figured this out. Occult practices, pagan religions, ancient sacrificial cults — they all demand blood. Especially innocent blood. Children. Because innocence carries more weight in the spiritual realm — the blood of an innocent victim is worth more.

They are operating on the same spiritual principle. They just have it inverted. They say: you owe a debt, so pay it with someone else's blood. Sacrifice to appease the power above you.

That should tell you something. The principle — blood pays the spiritual debt — is written into the fabric of reality. Multiple independent cultures and spiritual systems discovered it because it is actually true.

The question is: whose blood? And whose decision?

---

## God's Answer: The Cross

This is where Christianity is completely unlike every other system.

God did not demand your blood. God did not demand someone else's blood. God paid it Himself.

Jesus is the Son of God. The Bible teaches that God exists as three persons — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — one God, not three. The Son of God entered His own creation as a fully human being. Born to a woman. Grew up. Lived a real human life.

The Bible says:

> *"The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us."* (John 1:14)

Plain English: God became a human being and lived among us.

This matters legally. The debt is humanity's debt. A purely divine being who never became human could not stand in humanity's place. The substitute must be human to take the human debt.

But a purely human substitute has a problem too. Every human being has their own debt. You cannot pay someone else's debt when you are already in debt yourself. The substitute must be sinless — no debt of their own — and their life must be worth enough to cover the debt of all.

Jesus was fully human. He was also fully God. He lived a sinless life. He had no debt of His own.

The Bible says:

> *"He was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed... and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."* (Isaiah 53:5-6)

Plain English: He was hurt for the wrong things we did. He took our punishment. God placed all of our guilt on Him.

This was written 700 years before Jesus was born.

At the cross, God placed the full legal weight of every person's guilt onto Jesus. Jesus absorbed it completely. He died. Blood was shed. The debt was paid.

The Bible puts it simply:

> *"God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us."* (Romans 5:8)

Plain English: While we were still guilty, still living against God, He died for us.

This is the answer to the question: *why is God good if He allowed suffering?*

Because when the moment came, He did not stay above it. He entered it. He absorbed it. He paid the price Himself — for people who had turned against Him. No cruel God does that.

---

## Why He Rose From the Dead

Three days after His death, Jesus came back to life. This is not just a religious claim. Paul writes:

> *"He was seen of Cephas, then of the twelve: after that, he was seen of above five hundred brethren at once."* (1 Corinthians 15:5-6)

Plain English: More than 500 people saw Him alive after His death. Many of them were still alive when this was written.

Here is why the resurrection is the legal confirmation of everything:

The wages of sin is death. Jesus had no sin. Death had no legal claim on Him. He bore the sentence of others. Once He had absorbed it fully, there was nothing left to hold Him in death. His resurrection was not a miracle override — it was the correct legal outcome.

The Bible says He *"was raised again for our justification."* (Romans 4:25)

Plain English: He rose to prove that our debt was accepted as paid. The case is closed.

---

## Why You Cannot Fix It With Good Behavior

Most people think about morality like a scale. Put enough good things on one side, and it tips in your favor.

That is not how debt works.

If you commit a crime and then spend the next month doing charity work, does the crime disappear? No. The crime is still on record. A judge who dismissed charges because the defendant did something nice afterward would not be merciful — he would be corrupt.

Your sin is already on the record. New good behavior does not erase it. The debt already exists.

This is what every religion misses. They all say: do more, try harder, earn your way. They put the burden on you. But you cannot earn your way out of a debt you already owe. The debt requires payment, not improvement.

Christianity is the only worldview that says: the debt is real, you cannot pay it, and it has already been paid by someone else. All you have to do is receive it.

---

## What You Actually Have to Do

Two things. The Bible calls them repentance and faith.

**Repentance** does not mean feeling guilty. Everybody feels guilty. Repentance means *turning*. It means stopping the thing that got you into debt in the first place — living as your own moral authority, answering to no one. Turning means acknowledging that God is the authority and you are not.

**Faith** does not mean forcing yourself to believe something you are not sure about. It means *trusting*. Trusting that what Jesus did at the cross was real, and that it is enough — and that you are not going to try to add your own good behavior to it or earn it yourself.

A simple way to think about it: you are stuck. Someone reaches out a hand. Faith is taking the hand. Not analyzing the hand. Not explaining why you should not need a hand. Just taking it.

The Bible says:

> *"Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved."* (Acts 16:31)

Plain English: Trust in Jesus and you will be saved.

---

## What This Actually Means

The debt that was against you — your specific life, your specific record — was answered at the cross. The question is whether you will receive that answer.

You do not need to be good enough first. You were not good enough. That is the whole point.

You do not need to understand everything. Start where you are.

If you want to respond: be honest with God. Tell Him what you understand, where you stand, and that you want to turn toward Him and trust what Jesus did. Not a performance. Just an honest response to something that was actually done for you.

Then read the gospel of John. It is a short account written by someone who was there. Ask your real questions. Do not suppress them.

*"God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life."* (John 3:16)

Plain English: God loved you enough to pay the price Himself. Anyone who trusts in Jesus will not be permanently lost. They will live.

That is the gospel.
