# Gospel v4 Plan

## Structural Climax

The cross is the climax. The entire document builds a legal case that has only one possible resolution. Every section before the cross adds another brick to the case for guilt, for the justice requirement, and for the impossibility of self-payment. The cross is where the case resolves. The resurrection is the verdict. The response is the reader's answer.

## Four-Part Structure

### Part 1 — The Legal Problem

Purpose: establish God's authority, the nature of the original command, the legal reality of sin, the impossibility of overlooking guilt.

Sections:

1. God is Creator — His authority is sourced, not arbitrary. He owns what He made. This is why He has the right to set the standard. (Genesis 1:1, Psalm 24:1, John 1:3, Colossians 1:16-17)
2. Creation was good — what the reader sees today is not the design. The broken world is not the original. (Genesis 1:31, Genesis 1:26, Genesis 2:7)
3. The first law — before any human legal system, God gave a command with a stated consequence. This is the legal framework: command, boundary, consequence. (Genesis 2:16-17)
4. The fall — what the serpent offered was not a snack; it was moral autonomy. The act of eating was a legal violation (transgression) of an explicit command. Shame, hiding, and blame followed immediately. (Genesis 3, 1 John 3:4, Romans 5:12)
5. The Tree of Life block — why God expelled Adam and Eve from the garden: not only punishment, but to prevent sin from becoming eternal and irreversible. Mercy is embedded in the judgment. (Genesis 3:22-24, Revelation 22:1-2)
6. God's character: holy, righteous, just — these three attributes are why sin cannot be overlooked. A holy God does not share a nature with evil. A righteous God always does right. A just God cannot wave away real guilt. The combination means sin must be dealt with. (Isaiah 6:3, Deuteronomy 32:4, Psalm 9:8, Genesis 18:25)
7. The courtroom — name the roles plainly: Judge (God), Law (God's standard), Accuser (Satan — real, named, with a specific role in Scripture), Defendant (humanity — all guilty), Advocate (Jesus). Show Scripture for each. (Job 1:6-11, Zechariah 3:1, Revelation 12:10, 1 John 2:1, 1 Timothy 2:5-6)
8. The wages of sin is death — sin earns a specific payment. The payment is life. This is why blood matters in the Old Testament sacrificial system. Blood is life given. (Romans 6:23, Genesis 2:17, Leviticus 17:11, Hebrews 9:22)

Hard questions embedded here: "Who made God?" — answered briefly in the God-is-Creator section. "Why did God create a world where this could happen?" — answered in the free will and real-choice note in the Tree of Knowledge section.

### Part 2 — Why Nothing Else Solves It

Purpose: establish that the legal problem cannot be solved by human effort, and that no other worldview correctly diagnoses or resolves the problem.

Sections:

1. Good works cannot erase guilt — a parallel: a crime is not cancelled by charity done afterward. The crime is still on the record. Good future performance does not erase a past legal debt. This is the fundamental error in every works-based system. (Romans 3:20, Ephesians 2:8-9)
2. Other worldviews fail the diagnosis — treat this compactly, not as a separate essay. Atheism: cannot ground moral obligation, has no answer for guilt before a holy God. Islam: no substitutionary sacrifice in the gospel sense. Buddhism: no personal Creator-God, no legal guilt, no substitute. Hinduism: karma tracks actions but offers no once-for-all cancellation. New Age / spiritual experience: a changed feeling is not an objective change of legal standing. (Philippians 3:9, John 14:6)
3. Why the solution must come from outside — humanity cannot pay a debt it owes using the same currency the debt is owed in. A guilty person cannot pay for their own guilt and survive. The payment requires life. The payer would be destroyed. The solution must come from someone who can bear the penalty without being permanently destroyed by it. (Romans 5:6-8)

Hard questions embedded here: "Why can't God just forgive?" — answered in the just-judge logic: a judge who ignores crimes is corrupt, not merciful.

### Part 3 — The Only Solution

Purpose: present Jesus as the only logically and legally sufficient substitute. Build to the cross as the resolution of everything established in Parts 1 and 2.

Sections:

1. The Trinity and the incarnation — one God, three persons. The eternal Son of God entered His own creation as a fully human being. Why both natures are required legally: as man, He can stand in humanity's place; as God, His life has infinite worth sufficient to cover infinite debt. Neither alone is enough. (Deuteronomy 6:4, John 1:1, 14, Matthew 1:23, Philippians 2:5-8, Hebrews 1:3)
2. What love actually is — the biblical definition: not a feeling but a sacrifice. "Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends." The gospel surpasses even that: "while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us." This is not sentiment. This is the precise, costly act of a God who chose to pay what only He could pay. (John 15:13, Romans 5:8, 1 John 4:9)
3. The cross — the climax. Lay out every element of the legal case that has already been established, and show how the cross answers each one. Jesus was sinless — no debt of His own. God placed the entire guilt of humanity on Him. The just died for the unjust. Justice was satisfied. The door opened. This section carries the full weight of the document. (Isaiah 53:4-6, 2 Corinthians 5:21, 1 Peter 2:24, 1 Peter 3:18, 1 John 2:2, Romans 3:25-26)

### Part 4 — The Verdict and Response

Purpose: show the resurrection as legal seal on the verdict, explain justification, explain repentance and faith in plain terms, and call the reader to respond personally.

Sections:

1. The resurrection as verdict — death had no legal claim on Jesus because He had no sin. He could not be held by it. His resurrection is heaven's public verdict: the payment was accepted, the case is closed. He is alive and still intercedes. (Acts 2:24, Romans 4:25, 1 Corinthians 15:3-8, Hebrews 7:25, Romans 8:34)
2. What it means to be justified — standing in Christ's record, not your own. No condemnation. Not reduced sentence. The case dismissed because the debt was paid by someone else. The Zechariah 3 picture: filthy garments removed, clean robes given — not self-cleaned, provided by God. (Romans 8:1, Romans 5:1, Isaiah 61:10, Zechariah 3:1-5)
3. Repentance and faith — what they actually mean. Repentance is not feeling sorry; it is turning — a change of direction from self-authority to God-authority. Faith is not blind belief; it is trusting Jesus and His finished work rather than your own record. State each precisely. (Mark 1:15, Acts 16:31, Ephesians 2:8-9)
4. Call to action — direct, personal, concrete. The price was paid for this specific reader. The question is not general. It is personal: stand before God in your own record, or in Christ's. Read John. Pray honestly. Respond.

## Analogies Kept and Why

| Analogy | Where it appears | Why it is kept | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creator-ownership (manufacturer/architect) | God as Creator section | Makes the source of God's authority concrete before any theological claim is made | Clarity: answers "why does God get to set rules?" |
| Crime vs. charity (good works don't cancel guilt) | Part 2, works section | Universally understood. Makes the legal-debt point immediately clear | Clarity: answers "can't I just do better?" |
| Legal debt currency (can't pay with the same currency you owe in) | Part 2, outside-solution section | Makes the logical necessity of a substitute clear without theology | Clarity: answers "why did it have to be this way?" |
| Zechariah 3 filthy garments | Justification section | This is biblical imagery, not imposed analogy — it is Scripture making the point | Kept because it is in the text |

Analogies removed from v3:
- "Imagine you are watching the second half of a film" — narrative opener, removed
- Immortality-while-poisoned analogy (Tree of Life section) — kept as clarity parallel, but rewritten without story framing

## What Is Added vs Removed vs Restructured vs v3

### Added in v4

- Part 2 (Why Nothing Else Solves It) as a distinct section — v3 had this scattered through the middle sections; v4 consolidates it as a logical gate before the cross
- Explicit legal-case logic at the opening: moral judgment implies standard, standard implies law, law implies judge, guilt implies debt, debt implies payment — this sequence is named before the Genesis narrative begins
- Tighter "why couldn't it be any other way" argumentation in the substitute section
- The four key questions are now explicitly tracked through the structure

### Removed in v4

- Story-atmosphere language: "Imagine you are watching the second half of a film" (opening of v3) — cut entirely
- "Think about what that means" — pattern removed throughout
- "Think about what a devastating shift that is" — removed
- Devotional/emotional framing in the cross section — rewritten as verdict announcement
- Lengthy "why evil" section as a standalone — absorbed compactly where it fits the logic

### Restructured in v4

- Worldviews section: moved from after repentance/faith (v3 structure) to Part 2, before the cross — it belongs in the case for why the solution must come from outside
- Hard questions (who made God, can we trust the Bible, why evil): addressed briefly inside relevant sections rather than as standalone sections that interrupt the case
- The cross section is now the explicit structural climax, written to carry that weight
- Resurrection follows immediately after the cross as the verdict — not separated by other content
