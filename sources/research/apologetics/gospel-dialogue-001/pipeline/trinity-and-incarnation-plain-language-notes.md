# Trinity And Incarnation Plain Language Notes

## One God

Christianity does not teach three gods. It teaches one God.

The Shema says, “The LORD our God is one LORD” (Deuteronomy 6:4).

## Father, Son, Holy Spirit

The Bible names the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit together in one divine name: “in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost” (Matthew 28:19).

They are distinct persons, yet not separate gods.

## Jesus as God the Son in flesh

John says, “the Word was God” and “the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us” (John 1:1-3, 14).

That means Jesus is not a mere prophet or moral teacher. He is God the Son entering human history.

## Why incarnation matters legally

If Jesus were only a man, He could not bear the weight of sin for the world in the way Scripture describes.

If Jesus were only God and not man, He could not stand in humanity’s place.

The incarnation matters because Jesus is fully God and fully man. As man, He can represent us. As God, His life has infinite worth and authority.

## Why Jesus is more than a mere man

Jesus says, “I and my Father are one” (John 10:30).

Hebrews says the Son is “the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person” (Hebrews 1:3).

Philippians says He “made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant” (Philippians 2:5-8).

This is why the cross is not just a good man dying. It is God coming into His own creation to redeem sinners.
