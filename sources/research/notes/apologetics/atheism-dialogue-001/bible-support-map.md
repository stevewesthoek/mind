# Bible Support Map

## Purpose

This map tracks which biblical texts support the apologetic posture and framework, and how to use them without proof-texting.

## Key Passages

| Passage | Main Use | Translation / wording note | Greek / textual note | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Peter 3:15-16 | Apologetic posture: gentleness, respect, clear conscience | NIV: "with gentleness and respect" | BLB interlinear shows `ἀπολογία` (defense) and `πραΰτητος` / `πραΰτης` (gentleness/meekness) | Use as posture, not as a slogan. |
| 2 Timothy 2:24-26 | Patient correction, no quarrelsome speech | NIV: "must not be quarrelsome" | BLB interlinear shows `μάχεσθαι` (quarrel/strive), `ἤπιον` (kind/gentle), `διδακτικόν` (able to teach), `ἀνεξίκακον` (patient when wronged) | Use to restrain tone, not to weaken the rebuttal. |
| Romans 1:18-25 | General revelation / suppression of truth framework | NIV: truth is "suppressed" and God’s qualities are "clearly seen" in creation | BLB snippet highlights `κατεχόντων` (suppressing/holding down) and `ἀλήθειαν` (truth) | Do not turn this into a blanket psychological diagnosis of every atheist. |
| Psalm 19:1-4 | Creation as testimony / theological framing | NIV: heavens "declare" the glory of God | Hebrew-oriented use is poetic, not scientific | Do not use it to settle cosmology by itself. |
| Acts 17:22-31 | Public reasoning with non-Christians; creation, providence, repentance | NIV shows Paul engaging the Areopagus and starting from the altar to an unknown god | BLB interlinear on v.22 shows Paul addressing the Athens audience directly | Do not flatten the passage into mere agreement with pagan thought. |
| 1 Corinthians 15:3-8 | Early resurrection witness summary | NIV: "what I received I passed on" and list of appearances | Standard creed-like structure is central; use as early testimony, not automatic proof | Do not present it as if it ends historical debate by itself. |

## Translation Comparison Notes

- NIV is the best default for readability in the sendable letter.
- KJV/NKJV are useful for checking traditional phrasing.
- The Greek terms matter for posture:
  - `ἀπολογία` = defense / reasoned answer
  - `πραΰτης` / `πραΰτητος` = meekness / gentleness
  - `μάχεσθαι` = quarrel / fight
  - `ἀνεξίκακος` = patient when wronged / enduring evil

## Support Summary

These texts support:

- the tone of the reply
- the legitimacy of reasoned defense
- creation testimony
- public reasoning with skeptics
- early resurrection testimony

They do not support:

- overclaiming scientific certainty
- caricaturing atheists as stupid or rebellious
- treating unresolved questions as automatic proof
