# Pipeline V2 Raw Reaction Extraction

## Useful Direct Lines From Steve

- “The burden of proof is on the person making the extraordinary claim.”
- “You are still not excluded from explaining the metaphysical in another way.”
- “So what is your foundation then?”
- “That does not answer the question.”
- “If you cannot provide such a mechanism then atheism is factually falling like a house of cards.”
- “You are making the assumption that evolution is already proven.”
- “The premise does not prove the conclusion.”
- “That explains the method, not the worldview behind the method.”

## Useful Analogies

- Dragon-in-the-garage analogy: useful for default skepticism and burden of proof, but only if kept brief and tied to the actual point.
- House-of-cards analogy: useful for explaining how a worldview can collapse if its core support is missing.
- Football analogy: useful only in a limited way; it illustrates bare nonbelief, but it does not answer larger worldview questions.
- “We are all football players discovering the rules” line: useful only as a metaphor for deeper questions beyond the game.

## Useful Challenge Questions

- What is your foundation?
- Why should I accept your starting commitments as more than a philosophical preference?
- How do you ground logic, morality, meaning, and consciousness in impersonal matter?
- How does science ground its own method?
- If evolution does not explain the origin of life, what does?
- Why does the anthropic principle solve the causal question?
- How is “2,000-year-old hearsay” serious historical reasoning?

## Useful Logic / Fallacy Observations

- False equivalence: treating warranted trust as the same thing as blind faith.
- Straw man: turning the faith critique into “all trust is blind trust.”
- Circular reasoning: using naturalism to prove naturalism.
- Category error: treating science’s method as if it settled metaphysics.
- Non sequitur: “brain damage affects consciousness, therefore materialism.”
- Burden shift: requiring proof from Steve while making unsupported claims in return.
- False analogy: comparing atheism to not playing football without addressing worldview-level claims.
- Unsupported assertion: “naturalism is a conclusion from thousands of years of testing.”

## Claims Needing Source Support

- Atheism as a functional religion/worldview needs careful definitional support from philosophy and Scripture.
- The claim that science does not establish its own metaphysical starting points needs philosophy sources.
- Consciousness as ontologically underexplained needs hard-problem sources.
- The origin-of-life question being unresolved needs peer-reviewed scientific sources.
- Fine-tuning as an inference problem needs a philosophy/science source note.
- Resurrection as a cumulative historical case needs historical sources.

## Emotional / Raw Lines To Avoid In Public Response

- “Atheism is factually falling like a house of cards.”
- “This is a dishonest tactic.”
- “Why so defensive?”
- “You are just making definitions.”
- “Science and religion go hand in hand” if used as a broad slogan without explanation.
- “Theists are not anti-science” if used as a defensive aside instead of direct argument.
- Any line that sounds like ridicule, contempt, or personal attack.

## Material That Can Improve The Final Response

- First-person challenge framing: “So what is your foundation then?”
- Burden-of-proof pressure: “The premise does not prove the conclusion.”
- Concrete examples instead of abstraction.
- Short, direct analogies where they clarify the point.
- Plain-language phrasing of philosophy terms.
- The insistence that the opponent answer the actual question instead of just narrowing definitions.
