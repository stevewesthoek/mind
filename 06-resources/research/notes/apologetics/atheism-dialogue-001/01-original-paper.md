# **Is Atheism a Faith?**

A logical examination of atheism, scientism, evolution, and naturalistic worldviews.

This document examines whether atheism is truly a neutral position or whether it functions as a belief system grounded in philosophical assumptions.

The goal is not to attack individuals who call themselves atheists.

The goal is to examine **logical consistency**.

If a worldview rests on assumptions that cannot be proven, then that worldview requires **faith**.

---

# **Definitions**

Before discussing atheism, the core terms must be defined.

---

# **Faith**

Faith means **trust or belief in something that cannot be empirically proven**.

Every worldview contains foundational beliefs that cannot be scientifically demonstrated.

Examples include:

• the reliability of reason  
• the validity of logic  
• the uniformity of nature

These assumptions must be accepted before science can begin.

---

# **Religion**

A religion is a framework that explains:

• the origin of the universe  
• the nature of reality  
• morality  
• meaning and purpose  
• the destiny of humanity

If a worldview answers these questions, it functions as a **religious framework**, even if it does not call itself a religion.

---

# **Atheism**

Atheism is the belief that **God does not exist**.

Modern atheism normally rests on several philosophical assumptions:

• naturalism  
• materialism  
• evolution as the explanation of life  
• science as the ultimate authority of knowledge

These assumptions form a worldview.

---

# **Scientism**

Scientism is the belief that:

**Science is the only valid source of knowledge.**

Scientism is not a scientific statement.

It is a **philosophical claim about science**.

---

# **The Central Question**

If atheism depends on assumptions that cannot be scientifically proven, then atheism requires **faith**.

The question becomes: **Is atheism simply another belief system?**

# **The Argument Ladder**

When discussing atheism and naturalistic worldviews, it is important to follow the correct logical order.

Many become confused in their logic because arguments are discussed in the wrong sequence.

The questions of atheism, evolution, and science belong to **different levels of explanation**.

The correct order is:

Level 1 — **Metaphysics**  
 What kind of reality exists?

Level 2 — **Epistemology**  
 How can humans know truth?

Level 3 — **Origins**  
 How did life begin?

Level 4 — **Biological development**  
 How do organisms change over time?

Evolution belongs only to **Level 4**.

Therefore evolution cannot serve as the foundation for atheism.

Evolution itself depends on earlier assumptions about reality.

---

# **Level 1 — Metaphysical Foundation**

Atheism assumes that reality is entirely material.

Everything that exists must ultimately be explained by:

 • matter  
 • energy  
 • physical laws

However, several fundamental aspects of reality are **immaterial**:

 • mathematical truths  
 • logical laws  
 • consciousness  
 • moral values

If immaterial realities exist, strict materialism becomes impossible to sustain.

---

# **Level 2 — Epistemological Foundation**

If naturalism is true, human reasoning evolved through natural selection.

Natural selection favors behaviors that increase survival.

It does not necessarily favor **true beliefs**.

This creates a problem:

If human cognition evolved primarily for survival rather than truth, the reliability of human reasoning becomes uncertain.

Yet atheistic arguments rely heavily on human reasoning.

---

# **Level 3 — Origin of Life**

Before evolution can occur, life must already exist.

However, no experiment has demonstrated life emerging from non-life.

The first living cell requires:

 • genetic information  
 • molecular machinery  
 • complex organization

The origin of this system remains unresolved.

---

# **Level 4 — Biological Development**

Only after life exists can evolutionary mechanisms operate.

Evolution attempts to explain how organisms diversify over time.

However, several questions remain:

 • the origin of biological information  
 • the emergence of complex systems  
 • the existence of consciousness  
 • the reliability of evolved reasoning

These questions are examined later in this document.

# **10 Arguments Against Atheism**

These arguments challenge the internal logic of atheistic naturalism.

---

# **1 — The Beginning of the Universe**

Scientific evidence indicates the universe began to exist.

Evidence includes:

• Big Bang cosmology  
• expansion of space  
• thermodynamics

Everything that begins to exist requires a cause.

Therefore the universe requires a cause beyond itself.

The cause must be:

• timeless  
• spaceless  
• immaterial  
• powerful

These properties correspond to what is traditionally called **God**.

---

# **2 — Fine-Tuning of the Universe**

The constants of physics are precisely calibrated.

Examples:

• gravity  
• electromagnetic force  
• cosmological constant

Small variations would make life impossible.

Possible explanations:

• necessity (a triangle must have 3 sides)  
• chance  
• design

Necessity does not answer the ‘why’ and is therefore insufficient, chance is extraordinarily improbable and just kicks the can down the road and in the end also does not answer why the universe exists in the first place.

Design provides the most coherent explanation.

---

# **3 — The Origin of Life**

Life has never been observed to arise from non-life.

Major unresolved problems include:

• molecular organization  
• genetic coding  
• cellular machinery

Abiogenesis remains unsolved.

Belief that life arose spontaneously currently rests on **philosophical assumption rather than demonstrated mechanism**.

---

# **4 — Biological Information**

DNA contains vast amounts of coded information.

A single human cell contains billions of instructions.

In every known case:

**complex information originates from intelligence.**

---

# **5 — Consciousness**

Humans possess subjective consciousness.

This includes:

• awareness  
• intentional thought  
• self-reflection

Purely physical processes have never been shown to produce subjective experience.

---

# **6 — Objective Morality**

Most humans believe certain acts are objectively wrong:

• murder  
• rape  
• torturing children

If morality is only social preference, then these acts become merely **opinions**.

Objective moral law suggests a **moral lawgiver.**

---

# **7 — Rational Thought**

If human reasoning evolved purely for survival rather than truth, then our reasoning cannot be trusted.

This creates a dilemma:

If naturalism is true, the reasoning used to defend naturalism is **unreliable**.

---

# **8 — Mathematical Reality**

The universe operates according to mathematical laws.

Mathematics is:

• abstract  
• immaterial  
• universal

Material processes cannot explain **why** mathematical truths exist.

---

# **9 — Laws of Logic**

Logical laws such as:

• **non-contradiction** (a statement cannot be both true and false in the same sense at the same time)  
• **identity** (a thing is identical to itself; what something is, it is (A \= A))  
• **excluded middle** (a statement must be either true or false; there is no third option between the two)

are universal and immaterial.

Yet they govern all rational thought.

---

# **10 — The Historical Case for Jesus**

Historical facts accepted by many historians include:

• Jesus existed  
• he was crucified  
• the tomb was empty (from where his dead body arose)  
• His disciples and many others saw the risen Jesus for 40 days after His burial  
• skeptics converted  
• Christianity spread rapidly

The resurrection explains these facts more coherently than competing theories.

# **2 — Science Depends on Philosophy**

• laws of logic  
• mathematics

# **3 — Science Cannot Explain Why the Universe Exists**

Science explains how things behave.

It cannot explain **why anything exists at all**.

---

# 

# **4 — Science Cannot Ground Morality**

Science cannot determine what humans **ought to do**.

---

# **5 — Science Cannot Explain Consciousness**

Brain activity can be measured.

But subjective experience remains unexplained.

---

# **6 — Science Cannot Explain Meaning**

Questions about:

• purpose  
• meaning  
• value

are philosophical, not scientific.

---

# **7 — Science Cannot Prove Its Own Method**

The scientific method assumes principles that science itself cannot prove.

---

# 

# **8 — Science Cannot Measure Many Real Things**

Examples:

• love  
• beauty  
• justice  
• meaning

These are real yet not scientifically measurable.

---

# **9 — Science Cannot Explain Logic**

Science relies on logical laws but cannot explain their origin.

---

# **10 — Science Cannot Replace Philosophy**

Science answers **how questions**.

Philosophy answers **why questions**.

# **10 Arguments Against Evolution**

These arguments challenge the sufficiency of Darwinian evolution as a complete explanation of life.

---

# **1 — The Origin of Life**

Evolution assumes life already exists.

It does not explain how life originated from non-life.

---

# **2 — Missing Transitional Forms**

If evolution is gradual, nature should contain vast numbers of transitional species.

These forms are largely absent.

---

# **3 — Biological Information**

DNA contains complex coded information.

Information is known to originate from intelligence.

---

# **4 — Irreducible Complexity**

Certain biological systems require many parts to function simultaneously.

Examples include molecular machines inside cells.

Partial systems would be non-functional.

---

# **5 — Consciousness**

Evolution does not explain why consciousness exists.

Self-awareness offers no clear survival advantage.

---

# **6 — The Problem of Trustworthy Minds**

If evolution selects for survival rather than truth, then our beliefs are not reliable.

---

# **7 — The Information Problem**

Evolution explains modification of existing information.

It does not explain the origin of new complex information.

---

# **8 — Entropy**

The universe naturally moves toward disorder.

Evolution claims increasing complexity despite this principle.

---

# **9 — Lack of Universal Transitional Evidence**

If evolution shaped all life, transitional forms should exist throughout all species.

Instead, most species appear as distinct categories.

---

# **10 — Abiogenesis Dependency**

Evolution depends entirely on the existence of the first living cell.

Without explaining that event, the evolutionary framework remains incomplete.

# **10 Common Atheist Objections and Refutations**

---

# **Objection 1**

Science will eventually explain everything.

Refutation:

This is not science.

It is faith in future discoveries. Also truth always has the tendency to reveal itself since it is grounded in reality. It should therefore be reasonably easy to explain the purpose of life,  which it is, through the belief of an architect, creator or higher being that purposefully created the world we live in, including humanity.

---

# **Objection 2**

God is a “God of the gaps” (means because we do not understand, therefore God).

Refutation:

The arguments presented are **positive explanations**, not gaps.

---

# **Objection 3**

The multiverse explains fine-tuning.

Refutation:

The multiverse has no empirical evidence. Also it just kicks the can down the road because who made the multiverse or the universe that made our universe? It does not solve anything.

---

# **Objection 4**

Morality evolved.

Refutation:

Evolution may explain behavior.

It does not explain moral obligation.

---

# **Objection 5**

Religion causes violence.

Refutation:

Violence also occurs in secular regimes, if not more so.

---

# **Objection 6**

If God exists, why suffering?

Refutation:

This question assumes objective morality and atheists cannot explain their worldview in terms of bad, good and justification. If morality comes from man then any moral view is just someone (or some groups) opinion therefore, who is one person or group to say that other opinions are wrong.

---

# **Objection 7**

Religion is irrational.

Refutation:

Many arguments for theism are philosophical and logical. Christianity is grounded in evidence and logic. In the end all of humanity has to take a leap of faith since the truth is not in us but outside of us. May it be in God, naturalism or any other \-ism.

---

# **Objection 8**

Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence.

Refutation:

Historical events are evaluated using historical evidence and explanatory power, not laboratory repetition. The life, death, and reported resurrection of Jesus are supported by early sources, multiple testimonies, and the rapid rise of Christianity, which historians must explain.

---

# **Objection 9**

Atheism is simply lack of belief.

Refutation:

Modern atheism includes positive philosophical claims. Many atheists just have an issue with God rather than being honest truth seekers.

---

# **Objection 10**

Science disproves God.

Refutation:

Science studies natural processes.

God is not a natural object inside the universe. Also there is a lot of proof that points to a God. There is no proof for an alternative worldview that explains everything as well as God would.

---

Every worldview rests on foundational assumptions.

Atheism assumes:

• naturalism  
• materialism  
• unguided evolution  
• the sufficiency of science

None of these assumptions can be scientifically proven.

Therefore atheism requires **faith in philosophical assumptions**.

Then science proposes that:

• order emerged from chaos  
• life emerged from non-life  
• consciousness emerged from matter

At the very least, the atheist must admit that this worldview requires belief just like any other person of faith.

If that is not a religion then I do not know what is.

If one does not see the irony in this, then the issue may not be logic but a personal resistance to faith or religion.

---

Atheism also struggles to ground several fundamental aspects of human life.

Without God:

• morality becomes opinion  
• love becomes chemistry  
• truth becomes neural activity  
• meaning becomes illusion

If morality has no objective standard, then claims of good and evil are merely personal preferences.

If love is only chemical reactions, then its meaning disappears.

If reasoning is determined by brain chemistry alone, then the reliability of our thoughts becomes questionable.

If meaning, beauty, art and love are only by-products of matter, then existence becomes fundamentally empty.

Why pursue truth, justice, or sacrifice for others if everything is ultimately meaningless?

---

If evolution were fully true in the way it is often presented:

We should see clear gradual transitions across all species.

Instead, most animals appear as **distinct biological categories**.

Elephants remain elephants.

Giraffes remain giraffes.

Rhinos remain rhinos.

The evidence for continuous transitions across all life forms is not obvious in nature.

Evolutionary explanations often rely on **hypothetical transitions rather than direct observation**.

---

Another problem concerns consciousness.

Animals function effectively without self-awareness.

Yet humans possess deep consciousness, reflection, and existential questioning.

The evolutionary advantage of this capacity is not obvious.

---

Entropy presents another difficulty.

Everything in the universe naturally moves toward disorder.

Information can be reorganized but not spontaneously increased.

Selective breeding illustrates this principle.

Dogs originated from wolves.

Breeding produces smaller subsets of genetic information.

For example:

A chihuahua contains less genetic information than a wolf.

You cannot get a chihuahua from a wolf and all dog breeds have very specific birth defects or are prone to specific diseases due to their deteriorated gene pool.

Breeding (which is just controlled and accelerated “evolution”) removes information rather than creating it.

The question then becomes:

**What natural process adds new genetic information rather than degrading it?**

# **15 Questions Atheists Cannot Answer Without Borrowing From Theism**

These questions expose philosophical tensions within strict atheistic naturalism.

They are not rhetorical attacks.

They are logical questions about **foundations**.

---

# **1 — Why Does Anything Exist?**

Why does the universe exist rather than nothing?

Science can describe the universe.

It cannot explain why there is something rather than nothing.

---

# **2 — Why Do the Laws of Nature Exist?**

Physics describes laws governing reality.

But why do these laws exist at all?

And why are they mathematically elegant and stable?

---

# **3 — Why Does the Universe Follow Mathematics?**

The universe operates according to precise mathematical structures.

Why should physical reality obey abstract mathematical truths?

---

# **4 — Why Do Logical Laws Exist?**

Logic is:

 • universal  
 • immaterial  
 • necessary

Where do these laws come from?

Material objects cannot produce universal logical principles.

---

# **5 — Why Is the Universe Fine-Tuned for Life?**

The physical constants of the universe are precisely balanced for life.

Why does the universe permit life at all?

---

# **6 — Why Does Consciousness Exist?**

Matter has no awareness.

Yet humans possess subjective experience and self-reflection.

How can unconscious matter produce consciousness?

---

# **7 — Why Do Humans Seek Meaning?**

Humans naturally ask questions such as:

 • Why do we exist?  
 • What is our purpose?  
 • What is truth?

Why would a purposeless universe produce beings searching for purpose?

---

# **8 — Why Do Humans Believe in Objective Morality?**

Most humans believe some actions are objectively wrong:

 • murder  
 • rape  
 • torture

If morality is only a social construct, these claims become mere preferences.

---

# **9 — Why Is Human Life Valuable?**

If humans are accidental configurations of molecules, why do humans possess inherent value?

On a purely material level, a human being and a rock are simply arrangements of matter.

---

# **10 — Why Trust Human Reason?**

If human reasoning is the product of blind evolutionary processes aimed at survival rather than truth, why trust our reasoning?

This includes reasoning used to defend atheism.

---

# **11 — Why Does the Universe Contain Beauty?**

Humans recognize beauty in:

 • art  
 • music  
 • nature

Why would a purely material universe generate beings capable of perceiving beauty?

---

# **12 — Why Does Love Exist?**

Love is one of the most powerful human experiences.

If love is merely chemistry, why does it carry such profound meaning and moral weight?

---

# **13 — Why Do Humans Create Art?**

Art, music, poetry and storytelling appear throughout every human culture.

These activities often serve no survival function.

Why do humans create beauty for its own sake?

---

# **14 — Why Do Humans Desire Justice?**

Across cultures, humans instinctively desire justice.

People believe evil should be punished and good rewarded.

Why would such a universal moral intuition exist in a purely indifferent universe?

---

# **15 — Why Did Christianity Begin?**

Christianity began with a radical claim:

Jesus rose from the dead.

Within a few decades this belief spread across the Roman Empire despite persecution.

What event best explains this historical explosion?

# **Final Observation**

These questions do not automatically prove theism.

But they reveal something important.

Atheistic naturalism struggles to explain several fundamental aspects of reality.

A worldview must be able to account for:

 • the existence of the universe  
 • consciousness  
 • objective morality  
 • truth and rational thought  
 • meaning and purpose

The debate is therefore not between **faith and no faith**.

The debate is between **competing explanations of reality**.

Every worldview begins with foundational assumptions.

The question is not whether someone has faith.

The question is **which worldview best explains the evidence**.

Truth has a distinctive property.

If something is true, its effects become visible in reality.

Truth leaves traces.

It explains observations.

It produces coherence.

False explanations struggle to account for the evidence and must rely on additional assumptions.

If a theory is correct, its explanatory power should become clear when we examine the world honestly.

If evolution in the strongest naturalistic sense were fully sufficient to explain life, we would expect overwhelming and universally clear evidence demonstrating the continuous transformation of all life forms.

Yet many, if not all, of the key mechanisms remain debated and many foundational questions remain unresolved.

By contrast, the Christian worldview claims that reality was created by a rational mind.

If this were true, we would expect to find:

 • a universe governed by mathematical order  
 • moral awareness within human beings  
 • rational minds capable of discovering truth  
 • a deep human search for meaning and purpose

These features are precisely what we observe.

Christianity also claims that God revealed Himself in history through Jesus Christ.

This claim is connected to historical events that can be investigated and discussed.

The central question therefore becomes simple.

Which explanation better accounts for reality as we actually observe it?

When truth is examined honestly, the goal is not to defend a prior position.

The goal is to **follow the evidence wherever it leads**.

Truth does not fear investigation.

Truth reveals itself through coherence, explanatory power, and consistency with reality.

The challenge for every worldview is therefore the same:

**Which explanation best accounts for the world we live in?**
