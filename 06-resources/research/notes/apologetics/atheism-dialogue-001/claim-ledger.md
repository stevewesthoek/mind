## Steve Claims

| ID | Claim | Type | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | A worldview that rests on assumptions that cannot be proven requires faith. | philosophical, logical | mixed | Needs tighter definition of faith; this can become a definitional dispute. |
| S2 | Faith means trust in something that cannot be empirically proven. | definitional | weak | Too broad; collapses warranted trust and religious faith. |
| S3 | Atheism is the belief that God does not exist. | definitional | mixed | Accurate for some atheists, not for all; many define atheism as lack of belief. |
| S4 | Modern atheism normally rests on naturalism, materialism, evolution, and science as ultimate authority. | philosophical, definitional | weak | Overgeneralizes atheists and conflates atheism with broader naturalism. |
| S5 | Evolution belongs only to level 4 and cannot serve as the foundation for atheism. | logical, definitional | mostly sound | Good distinction, but the argument should acknowledge that many atheists do not treat evolution as a foundation anyway. |
| S6 | Atheism assumes reality is entirely material. | philosophical | weak | This describes materialism/naturalism more than atheism itself. |
| S7 | Mathematical truths, logical laws, consciousness, and moral values are immaterial. | philosophical, scientific, ethical | mixed | The immaterial status of math and logic is debated; morality and consciousness require more careful argument. |
| S8 | If immaterial realities exist, strict materialism becomes impossible to sustain. | philosophical | mixed | Depends on what counts as "materialism" and whether abstract objects count as "things." |
| S9 | Natural selection favors survival, not truth, so evolved cognition is unreliable. | philosophical, scientific | mixed | This is a real argument, but it is overstated without nuance. |
| S10 | No experiment has demonstrated life emerging from non-life. | scientific | mostly sound | True in a strict laboratory sense, but the conclusion should remain modest. |
| S11 | The first living cell requires genetic information, molecular machinery, and complex organization. | scientific | mostly sound | Reasonable summary, but it does not by itself imply design. |
| S12 | Everything that begins to exist requires a cause. | logical, philosophical | mixed | Strong as a metaphysical principle, but not a settled scientific law. |
| S13 | The universe began to exist, so it requires a cause beyond itself. | philosophical, scientific | mixed | Depends on cosmology and on whether "beginning" is the right concept. |
| S14 | Fine-tuning best supports design. | philosophical | mixed | Plausible inference, but alternatives need more care. |
| S15 | DNA contains coded information and information originates from intelligence. | scientific, philosophical | mixed | Needs clarification of "information"; the conclusion is not automatic. |
| S16 | Consciousness has not been shown to arise from physical processes. | scientific, philosophical | mostly sound | Better framed as "not fully explained" rather than "not shown at all." |
| S17 | Objective morality suggests a moral lawgiver. | philosophical, ethical | mixed | Classic moral argument; conclusion is not compelled by the premise alone. |
| S18 | Scientism is self-refuting. | logical, philosophical | mostly sound | Stronger than many other claims, if scientism is defined as science-only truth. |
| S19 | Science cannot explain why the universe exists, morality, consciousness, meaning, or logic. | philosophical | mixed | Often true if "science" is used narrowly, but this is not a knockdown against naturalism. |
| S20 | Evolution does not explain abiogenesis. | scientific | sound | Correct category distinction. |
| S21 | Transitional fossils are absent or largely absent. | scientific | weak | False or at least badly overstated. |
| S22 | Entropy makes evolution problematic. | scientific | weak | Misuses the second law of thermodynamics. |
| S23 | Breeding only removes information. | scientific | weak | Overstated and often false in this form. |
| S24 | The resurrection explains the historical facts better than alternatives. | historical, theological | mixed | This is a legitimate apologetic claim but requires careful historical framing. |
| S25 | Atheism becomes a religion because it answers origin, morality, meaning, and destiny questions. | definitional, rhetorical | weak | Overbroad; "functionally worldview-like" is safer than "religion." |
| S26 | Science tells us the universe began from nothing. | scientific | weak | Overstates cosmology and risks conflating "Big Bang" with "from nothing." |
| S27 | Atheists resist faith or religion personally. | personal, rhetorical | weak | Unfair and not evidentially grounded. |

## Atheist Claims

| ID | Claim | Type | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Most atheists mean lack of belief, not belief that no god exists. | definitional | sound | Strong correction of Steve's framing. |
| A2 | The burden of proof is on the person making the god claim. | logical | sound | Good rebuttal to overconfident theistic claims. |
| A3 | Science is self-correcting and grounded in observation, not faith. | scientific, philosophical | mostly sound | Strong, though "science is self-correcting" is a process claim, not a total answer. |
| A4 | Naturalism is a conclusion from evidence rather than a starting faith position. | philosophical | mixed | Often true for working scientists, but naturalism can also function as a philosophical commitment. |
| A5 | Math and logic are descriptive tools, not magical entities. | philosophical | mixed | Helpful correction, though it does not settle their ontological status. |
| A6 | Morality can be explained biologically and socially. | ethical, scientific | mixed | Plausible partial explanation, not a complete moral theory. |
| A7 | Consciousness is tightly correlated with brain states. | scientific | sound | Strong empirical point, though correlation is not a full explanation. |
| A8 | Abiogenesis is not evolution. | scientific, definitional | sound | Important category distinction. |
| A9 | Transitional fossils are abundant. | scientific | sound | Correct and important. |
| A10 | The second law of thermodynamics does not rule out local increases in order on Earth. | scientific | sound | Correct. |
| A11 | Atoms and chemistry can explain many emergent properties without invoking souls. | scientific, philosophical | mixed | Good point, though not exhaustive. |
| A12 | Revelation is inconsistent across religions and thus unreliable. | philosophical, religious | mixed | Strong critique of private revelation, weaker as a universal dismissal of religion. |
| A13 | Many of Steve's arguments are straw men or false dilemmas. | logical | mixed | Some are, but not all. |
