# Pipeline V2 Hypocrisy And Inconsistency Ledger

## Clear Double Standards

| ID | Double standard | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| H001 | The respondent says Steve’s worldview needs proof, but treats naturalism as a default conclusion from testing without proving metaphysical naturalism. | He applies a higher standard to theism than to his own worldview. |
| H002 | The respondent says faith is bad when Steve uses the term broadly, but then treats his own trust in induction, uniformity, and reason as if they need no philosophical defense. | He criticizes starting commitments while relying on them himself. |
| H003 | The respondent rejects "unresolved science = God," but repeatedly treats "unresolved science = naturalism by default." | That is an inconsistency in explanatory burden. |
| H004 | The respondent says extraordinary claims need extraordinary evidence, but treats the historical testimony around Jesus as automatically dismissed because it is ancient. | He does not apply the historical standard evenly. |
| H005 | The respondent accuses Steve of faith-based worldview thinking while implicitly relying on philosophical commitments about logic, reason, and truth-tracking cognition that he does not justify. | This is a worldview-level inconsistency, even if not intentional hypocrisy. |

## Where To Use “Hypocrisy” Carefully

- Only use hypocrisy when the double standard is clear and obvious.
- Otherwise use "inconsistency" or "double standard."

## Best Framing

- The opponent is not just wrong; he is applying one standard to theism and another to his own naturalism.
- The opponent’s own claims about science, reason, and truth-tracking cognition need justification too.
