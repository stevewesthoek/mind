## 1. Historical Case for Jesus

The historical/apologetics material in the proof-for-Jesus source is built around a cumulative case:

- Jesus existed as a historical figure.
- Jesus was crucified.
- The tomb was later reported empty.
- The disciples changed dramatically.
- Skeptics such as James and Paul became believers.
- The resurrection creed in 1 Corinthians 15 is treated as early witness material.
- Fulfilled prophecy is presented as part of the case.
- Miracles are treated as possible if God exists.
- Naturalism is treated as the deeper worldview issue behind resistance to miracles.

## 2. Resurrection Case

The resurrection material is not presented as a single knockdown proof. It is presented as a cumulative historical case:

- crucifixion is a strong historical fact
- early resurrection proclamation is important
- empty tomb and appearances are treated as part of the inference
- skeptics’ conversion is treated as relevant evidence
- the disciples’ transformation is treated as explanatory pressure
- the best explanation claim is central

## 3. Miracles / Supernatural Case

The source argues that miracles are not ruled out if God exists. It treats naturalism as a worldview commitment, not a neutral default. The key point is that a miracle is only impossible if one already assumes a closed natural system.

## 4. Legal Gospel Framework

The gospel-legal framework source uses a courtroom analogy:

- God is Creator
- God is holy and righteous
- sin is lawbreaking
- guilt brings judgment
- works cannot pay the debt
- Jesus is the substitute
- blood and payment language point to the cost of sin
- resurrection vindicates Jesus
- repentance and faith are the response
- salvation is grace, not earned merit

## 5. Questions and Doubts the Person May Have

The case materials anticipate questions about:

- who God is
- why Jesus had to die
- why blood matters
- why God cannot just forgive
- why sin is serious
- why other religions are false
- why spiritual experiences do not settle truth
- who made God
- why God allowed the serpent
- why evil exists
- which Bible translation to read
- what it means to be saved

## 6. Raw Material That Should Be Used Carefully

Use carefully:

- the legal/courtroom frame, because it is strong but can become mechanical if not explained
- blood/payment language, because it is biblical but can sound harsh if not explained
- prophecy claims, because they need careful wording and can be overstated
- miracle claims, because they should remain tied to the resurrection case and worldview discussion
