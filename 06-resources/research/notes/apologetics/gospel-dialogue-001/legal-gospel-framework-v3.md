# Legal Gospel Framework

## 1. Moral law

People make moral judgments every day. We say things are right, wrong, fair, cruel, unjust, or good.

If there is morality outside us, then there is a standard outside us. If there is a standard outside us, then morality is not just personal taste. It points to something real and binding.

**Courtroom analogy:** a moral standard works like law. Law is not just preference; it tells us what is allowed and what is forbidden.

**Biblical support:** Romans 2:14-16; Romans 3:19-20

**How to explain it to a seeker:** you already know some things are genuinely wrong, not merely unpopular. That sense of “ought” points beyond opinion.

## 2. Lawgiver and authority

If there is law, there is authority. If there is authority, there is a rightful Judge.

God is not judged by the law. God is the source of the law. His law flows from His character.

**Courtroom analogy:** a judge does not invent justice on the spot. The judge applies justice because the court has rightful authority.

**Biblical support:** Isaiah 33:22; James 4:12

**How to explain it to a seeker:** if morality is real, it did not invent itself. It points to the character of the One who made us.

## 3. Sin as lawbreaking

Sin is not only a mistake. Sin is guilt before God. Sin is rebellion against rightful authority. Sin breaks what is good.

**Courtroom analogy:** lawbreaking creates guilt. Guilt is not a mood; it is a real moral condition.

**Biblical support:** Romans 3:23; 1 John 3:4

**How to explain it to a seeker:** sin is not just “I slipped.” It is “I crossed the line God set.”

## 4. Courtroom picture

God is Judge. Satan is the accuser. Conscience bears witness. The law exposes guilt. Every person stands accountable.

No one can bribe the court with good works. Good deeds matter, but they do not erase guilt.

**Courtroom analogy:** when the case is heard, no one can hide behind reputation, effort, or excuses.

**Biblical support:** Job 1:6-12; Zechariah 3:1-4; Romans 2:15-16

**How to explain it to a seeker:** you do not stand before God on the basis of comparison with other people. You stand before Him on the basis of truth.

## 5. Why God cannot simply ignore sin

A judge who ignores evil is not righteous. If God ignored sin, He would not be holy and just.

Justice requires a real answer to guilt. That is why death, judgment, and payment language appear in Scripture.

**Courtroom analogy:** a judge who waves away evil is not compassionate; he is corrupt.

**Biblical support:** Exodus 34:6-7; Romans 3:25-26

**How to explain it to a seeker:** forgiveness is not the same thing as pretending the crime never happened.

## 6. The wages of sin is death

Wages means payment. Sin earns death. Death is not random in this framework; it is judgment.

**Courtroom analogy:** the sentence matches the offense. Sin is not small, so the consequence is not small.

**Biblical support:** Romans 6:23; Genesis 2:17

**How to explain it to a seeker:** the Bible is saying sin really costs something. It is not cheap.

## 7. Jesus as substitute and advocate

Jesus lived without sin. He had no guilt of His own. He could stand in the place of sinners.

Jesus died as a substitute. He paid the debt sinners could not pay. He is Advocate, Mediator, Savior, and Lord.

**Courtroom analogy:** the guilty need someone who can speak for them and pay what they cannot pay.

**Biblical support:** 2 Corinthians 5:21; Hebrews 4:15; 1 Timothy 2:5; 1 John 2:1

**How to explain it to a seeker:** Jesus did not only teach the way; He paid the debt.

## 8. Blood and payment

Blood means life given. The price of sin is life. Jesus gave His life willingly.

This is why blood language appears in the gospel. It is justice and love meeting at the cross.

**Courtroom analogy:** a payment is made, not by the guilty cleaning themselves up, but by the substitute giving what is needed.

**Biblical support:** Hebrews 9:22; Leviticus 17:11; Isaiah 53:5-6

**How to explain it to a seeker:** blood is not meant to sound strange for its own sake. It means life was given in your place.

## 9. Resurrection as vindication

Death had no rightful claim on sinless Jesus. The resurrection shows Jesus was vindicated.

The resurrection confirms the payment was accepted. It means Jesus is alive, not merely remembered.

**Courtroom analogy:** resurrection is God’s public verdict that Jesus is who He said He is.

**Biblical support:** 1 Corinthians 15:3-8; Romans 4:25

**How to explain it to a seeker:** if Jesus stayed dead, the case would be incomplete. If He rose, the cross was not a failure.

## 10. Repentance and faith

Repentance means turning from sin toward God. Faith means trusting Jesus, not your own works.

Salvation is not earned. Grace means undeserved mercy. The right response is to come to Jesus honestly.

**Courtroom analogy:** the sinner does not argue for innocence by self-improvement; the sinner asks for mercy on the basis of Christ.

**Biblical support:** Ephesians 2:8-9; Mark 1:15; Acts 2:38

**How to explain it to a seeker:** repentance is not self-hatred. It is a real turning. Faith is not pretending; it is relying on Jesus.
