# Final Follow-Up Letter V6 Review

## Overall assessment

This version moves the gospel explanation farther back into Genesis and makes the fall, incarnation, and resurrection more explicit. It remains structured and explanatory rather than homiletic.

## Strengths

- Genesis context is now clear from the beginning.
- The fall is explained as disobedience, shame, separation, and death.
- The legal framework is still the bridge, not the replacement story.
- The Trinity and incarnation are explained in plain language without getting overly technical.
- The resurrection’s legal meaning is much stronger and more explicit.
- The hard questions are handled in a controlled appendix-style section instead of derailing the main flow.
- Scripture is woven into the explanation throughout, not just listed at the end.

## Tone check

- The document avoids homiletic tone.
- The document avoids devotional framing.
- The document avoids a dry textbook feel by staying narrative and explanatory.
- The tone stays factual, direct, and serious.

## Structure and flow

- The structure is readable and logical.
- The movement from creation to fall to law to court to cross to resurrection is strong.
- The final invitation stays direct without becoming manipulative.

## Genesis and fall

- This is materially better than the v4 starting point.
- Eden now frames the need for law and redemption instead of treating the cross as the only starting point.
- The garden command and the tree of life sections are helpful and grounded in the text.

## Trinity and incarnation

- The explanation is simple enough for a seeker, while still being doctrinally accurate.
- It avoids excess technical language.
- It makes clear why the incarnation matters legally and representationally.

## Hard questions

- The main letter now gives concise answers to evil, Satan, suffering, and God’s judgment.
- The appendix map is the right place for the longer-form objections.
- The risk is only length, not topic inclusion.

## Resurrection legal meaning

- Stronger than previous versions.
- The letter now says more clearly that resurrection is vindication, accepted payment, and ongoing mediation.
- That is the right theological and legal emphasis.

## Possible cautions

- The letter is getting long enough that a future sendable version may need trimming.
- The comparative religion section is fair, but should stay broad and not drift into polemics.
- The old-earth / Satan-fall passages are mentioned carefully; that caution should remain.

## Ready status

- This is a strong candidate for replacement of v4 as the next final export draft.
- If Steve wants it even tighter, the next pass should trim repetition, not change the framework.
