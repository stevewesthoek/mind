# Pastoral Tone Notes

## Tone For This Person

- not aggressive
- not manipulative
- not debate-first
- not trying to win an argument
- clear call to respond
- honest about sin and judgment
- direct enough to avoid hiding the cost of following Christ

## Voice — Version 4 Direction

The v4 direction is factual, logical, and case-building. The voice is that of a lawyer laying out a case, not a preacher telling a story.

- direct and declarative
- explanatory, not narrative
- serious, not warm-fuzzy
- biblical and precise
- not churchy unless terms are explained immediately

## What Changes in v4

- Remove narrative framing: no "Imagine...", no "Think about...", no "Picture..."
- Remove story-atmosphere language: no lingering illustrations, no mood-building
- Analogies serve clarity only. An analogy is introduced, used to make one concept clear, and the writing moves on. Analogies do not set tone or atmosphere.
- The case must build logically. Each section earns its place by advancing the legal argument.
- The cross is the climax of the document structurally and logically. Everything before it builds the case. Everything after it is verdict and response.

## What Stays

- Clear call to respond
- Honest about sin and judgment
- Respectful and not harsh in tone
- Explain terms before pressing conclusions
- Keep the gospel central

## Practical Guidance

- Invite questions.
- Explain before pressing.
- Keep the gospel central.
- Do not minimize repentance, faith, or the cross.
- Do not let the explanation drift into devotional or narrative writing once the case is building.
