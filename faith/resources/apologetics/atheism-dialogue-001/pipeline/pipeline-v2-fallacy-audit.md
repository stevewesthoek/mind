# Pipeline V2 Fallacy Audit

## Identified Problems

- Straw man: the respondent repeatedly narrows Steve’s broader worldview critique into a bare "lack of belief" posture and then claims victory.
- Equivocation: "atheism" shifts between bare nonbelief and metaphysical naturalism; "faith" shifts between blind faith and warranted trust; "information" shifts between semantic code and chemistry.
- Category error: the respondent treats methodological success in science as if it settled metaphysical naturalism.
- False dilemma: the respondent frames the issue as science vs religion or evidence vs faith, ignoring that Steve’s argument is about worldview grounding.
- Non sequitur: "brain damage affects consciousness, therefore materialism" does not follow.
- Burden shifting: the respondent says Steve must disprove naturalism while making unsupported claims of his own.
- Appeal to ridicule: dragon-in-garage analogies, "mega-faith," "logic trap," and "puddle" moves function as ridicule, not argument.
- Unsupported assertion: "naturalism is a conclusion from thousands of years of testing" is asserted, not shown.
- Overgeneralization: the respondent treats one or two weak Steve formulations as if they collapse the broader case.
- Motive fallacy: claims that Steve is retreating, using gotchas, or declaring miracles from ignorance attack motive rather than argument.
- Genetic fallacy: dismissal of Scripture or revelation by origin or misuse rather than the claim itself.
- Motte-and-bailey: the respondent uses a narrow atheism definition for safety, then imports a broader naturalistic confidence when convenient.
- Special pleading: the respondent treats unfinished scientific questions as disqualifying for theism while treating his own naturalistic assumptions as exempt.
- Inconsistency: the respondent demands careful evidence for theism but repeatedly offers sweeping counterclaims without equivalent support.

## Where The Fallacy Is Strongest

- logic trap / gotcha language
- "2,000-year-old hearsay"
- "mega-faith"
- "naturalism is just a conclusion from testing"
- "brain damage proves consciousness is physical"

## What To Say Plainly

- That is not what Steve argued.
- That conclusion does not follow.
- This is an assertion, not an argument.
- That is a category error.
- That is a motive accusation, not a rebuttal.
