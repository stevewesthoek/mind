## Assistant
Yes, but not as "a skill."

Sell it as a cost-control operating system for AI coding, not as a GitHub-style skill pack.

Why: the "skills" space is already noisy and increasingly commoditized. There are public collections with hundreds or thousands of skills spanning Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor, and others, so "here is another skill" is weak positioning.

What is actually valuable is this:

- model selection logic
- cost reduction
- fallback routing
- practical setup across tools
- opinionated defaults that save money without wrecking output quality

That is a product. A raw skill file is not.

My take: 7.5/10 product potential, 4/10 as a standalone €29 "skill" page, 8.5/10 if packaged as a tiny system with proof.

The market logic is real. Google still offers free access paths and free-tier usage for parts of Gemini, with limits and rate caps depending on model/tier, and Gemini CLI itself now includes auto-routing between lighter and stronger models. OpenAI's ChatGPT plans also explicitly differentiate access and usage levels, including expanded Codex usage on paid plans. That means users already feel the pain of model choice and spend control.

So the pain is real. Your angle is valid.
But here is the blind spot:

People do not buy routing. They buy lower monthly cost, fewer bad calls, and a setup that stops them bleeding money.

That means your offer should be framed like this:

Best version
- Product name: AI Cost Router
- Promise: Use the cheapest model that can do the job without tanking quality
- Outcome: Better coding results at a fraction of normal monthly spend
- Buyer: indie hackers, vibe coders, small agencies, heavy Claude/Codex users

Not this:
- "advanced model router skill"
- "superpowers for Claude/Gemini/Codex"
- "inspired by advisor"

That language is nerd bait. Nerds steal. Buyers need a business outcome.

What I would sell:

1. Core product at €29
   - installable router files
   - opinionated routing rules
   - setup docs for Gemini + Claude + Codex
   - recommended plan stack
   - "use this task with this model" matrix
   - example configs
   - 5–10 real routing recipes

2. Better product at €79
   - everything above
   - benchmark examples
   - "before/after monthly cost" examples
   - stack-specific presets for Next.js/SaaS/dev workflows
   - troubleshooting guide
   - updates

3. Upsell at €149–€199
   - install help
   - one personalized config
   - "send me your workflow, I tune the router"

That is the ladder. The €29 entry works only if it is dead simple.

What will make or break it:

You need proof.
Without proof, this is just another AI accessory.
You need at least three hard artifacts:

- a side-by-side cost comparison
- a quality comparison by task
- a live demo showing routing decisions in practice

Example proof:
- "Simple repo mapping → Gemini free"
- "Architectural reasoning → Claude"
- "surgical patching → Codex"
- "Result: same workflow, materially lower spend"

That is what sells.

What I would not do:

- Do not compare yourself heavily to random GitHub skill packs. That drags you into commodity-land.
- Do not overemphasize "advanced." Buyers hear "complicated."
- Do not sell to everyone using AI. Sell to people already paying too much.
- Do not make the funnel about features first. Lead with monthly savings and better defaults.

Your best angle on X/Facebook is not:
- "Buy my router skill"

It is:
- "Most people are using the wrong model for the wrong task and paying for it."
- "I built a routing system that offloads cheap work to Gemini and keeps expensive reasoning where it matters."
- "Here's the exact stack."
- "Here's what it costs."
- "Here's the result."

That content can convert.

Best funnel structure:

- Headline: Stop burning money on AI coding tools
- Subhead: A routing setup for Claude, Codex, and Gemini that pushes cheap tasks to cheap models and keeps premium reasoning for work that actually needs it
- Section 1: what problem it solves
- Section 2: what gets routed where
- Section 3: real cost example
- Section 4: what's included
- Section 5: setup time
- Section 6: who it's for / not for
- CTA: Get the router

Big risk:
This can be copied.
So the moat is not the raw files. The moat is:
- documentation
- defaults
- tested recipes
- updates
- credibility
- demos
- audience trust

That means this is a decent cashflow micro-product, not a fortress business.

So the verdict:

Yes, sell it.
But sell it as a small outcome-focused system, not as a "skill."
And do not stop at the file. Package the judgment.

Single best move from here:
Build a one-page funnel plus one brutal demo: "I replaced an expensive all-Claude workflow with a routed stack." Then sell the config + docs bundle.
